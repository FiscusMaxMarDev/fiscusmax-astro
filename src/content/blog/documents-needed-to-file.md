Filing season stress rarely comes from the tax law itself — it comes from scrambling to find a document at 11pm the night before a deadline. Having the right paperwork organized before you sit down to file, or hand things off to a preparer, is the single biggest factor in whether filing season feels smooth or chaotic. Here's what actually belongs on that list.

## Income Documents

Start with anything that reports income: W-2s from employers, 1099-NECs or 1099-Ks for contract and gig work, 1099-INTs and 1099-DIVs for interest and dividends, 1099-Rs for retirement distributions, and K-1s if you have an interest in a partnership, S-corp, or trust. If you had income from more than one source this year — a job change, a side hustle, freelance work — expect more forms than usual, and don't assume they'll all arrive at the same time. Some issuers are notoriously late, so it's worth keeping your own running list of income sources rather than relying purely on what shows up in the mail or your inbox.

## Deduction and Credit Documentation

On the deduction side, gather mortgage interest statements, property tax records, receipts for charitable donations, medical expense records if they were significant, and childcare or education expense statements. Business owners need a full accounting of expenses, mileage logs, and receipts for anything claimed as a deduction — the IRS has little patience for deductions with no paper trail behind them. If you contributed to an HSA, IRA, or 529 plan, those statements matter too, since contributions can affect your return even though the money didn't come from an employer.

## Personal Information That Trips People Up

Social Security numbers for every dependent, bank account and routing numbers for direct deposit, and last year's return for reference are all easy to forget until you're already mid-filing. If anything changed — a marriage, a divorce, a new address, a name change — make sure the documentation reflects it, because mismatches between what you report and what's on file elsewhere are one of the most common reasons a return gets flagged for review.

## Build the Habit Now, Not in April

The families and business owners who file with the least stress aren't the ones with the simplest tax situations — they're the ones who kept a folder, physical or digital, running all year long. Starting that habit today, even mid-year, beats trying to reconstruct twelve months of documents from memory in March.

> Do you have everything you need before filing season hits, or will you be piecing it together at the last minute again this year? A little organization now prevents a lot of stress later — and a lot of costly mistakes.
