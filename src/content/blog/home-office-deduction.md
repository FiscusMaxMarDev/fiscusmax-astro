The home office deduction has a reputation as an audit magnet, which causes plenty of business owners who genuinely qualify to skip it out of fear rather than lost benefit. Used correctly, with the right documentation behind it, it's a legitimate deduction that reflects real business costs — not a red flag waiting to happen.

## What "Regular and Exclusive Use" Actually Means

To qualify, a space in your home generally needs to be used regularly and exclusively for business — a spare room that doubles as a home office during the day and a guest room on weekends technically fails the exclusive-use test, even if the business use is genuine and significant. The space doesn't need to be an entire separate room; a clearly defined portion of a room can qualify, as long as that specific area isn't also serving a personal purpose. This is the detail most likely to disqualify an otherwise legitimate claim, and it's worth being honest about before claiming the deduction rather than discovering the issue during a review.

## Two Ways to Calculate It

The simplified method applies a flat rate per square foot of qualifying space, up to a set maximum, and requires minimal recordkeeping. The regular method instead calculates the actual percentage of your home used for business and applies that percentage to real expenses — mortgage interest or rent, utilities, insurance, and depreciation. The regular method usually produces a larger deduction for homeowners with significant home-related expenses, but it also requires substantially more documentation. Business owners with a modest space or simpler finances often find the simplified method's ease outweighs the extra dollars the regular method might otherwise produce.

## What It Signals About Overall Deductions

Claiming the home office deduction can also unlock the ability to deduct a portion of home-related expenses tied to business use more broadly, and it can support the deductibility of mileage for business trips that start and end at a qualifying home office rather than a separate business location. It's not an isolated line item — it connects to how several other deductions are calculated, which is part of why it's worth understanding thoroughly rather than skipping it reflexively out of audit anxiety.

## Documentation Beats Fear

A few photos of the space, a simple floor plan showing the percentage of the home involved, and consistent recordkeeping year over year do more to protect a legitimate claim than avoiding the deduction altogether. The deduction isn't the risk — an undocumented, exaggerated, or dishonest claim is.
