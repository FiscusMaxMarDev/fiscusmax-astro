Retirement plans are usually framed as a savings decision, but for business owners they're just as much a tax decision — often one of the largest deductions available that doesn't require spending money on anything outside your own future. The right plan can shelter a substantial amount of income every year, and most owners are using a plan sized for a much smaller business than the one they actually run.

## SEP IRAs Are the Simplest Upgrade

A SEP IRA lets a business owner contribute a percentage of income, up to a meaningfully higher limit than a typical employee retirement account, with minimal paperwork and no annual filing requirement in most cases. It's often the first upgrade owners make once profit outgrows what a standard IRA can shelter, precisely because it requires so little administrative overhead relative to the size of the deduction it unlocks. The tradeoff is that if you have employees, contributions generally need to be made proportionally on their behalf too, which changes the math for businesses with staff.

## Solo 401(k)s Often Beat a SEP for Owner-Only Businesses

For a business with no employees other than the owner and possibly a spouse, a solo 401(k) frequently allows for higher total contributions than a SEP IRA at the same income level, because it lets the owner contribute both as an employee and as the employer. It also often allows a Roth option, which a SEP typically doesn't, giving owners a choice between an upfront deduction now or tax-free withdrawals later. The added flexibility comes with slightly more setup and reporting once the account grows past a certain size.

## Defined Benefit Plans for High, Steady Earners

For business owners with high, consistent profit and a desire to shelter significantly more than a SEP or solo 401(k) allows, a defined benefit plan can permit substantially larger contributions, calculated actuarially based on age and target retirement income. These plans come with real complexity and ongoing administrative costs, which makes them worth considering mainly for owners further along in their careers with the income to justify the setup.

## The Plan Should Match the Business, Not the Other Way Around

Too many owners default to whatever plan they set up in their first profitable year and never revisit it as income grows. The gap between the plan you have and the plan that actually fits your current profit level is, in effect, a deduction you're leaving unclaimed every single year.
